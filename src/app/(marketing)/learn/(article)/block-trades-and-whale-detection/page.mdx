A **block trade** is a single execution that is unusually large relative to a market's
normal trade size. Spotting them is one of the most popular uses of order flow — and one
of the easiest to do badly.

## What makes a print a "block"

There's no universal number. A 5 BTC print is routine on BTCUSDT and enormous on a thin
altcoin. The right definition is **relative**: a trade whose **notional** (price ×
quantity) sits far above the typical size for *that* symbol on *that* venue.

That's why a good detector uses a **per-symbol notional threshold**, not one global number.
Order Flow Matrix sets a sensible default per symbol and lets you override the threshold to
match what you consider "large."

## Why traders care

Large prints can mark:

- **Conviction** — a sizable participant taking a position decisively.
- **Absorption** — big aggressive prints that *fail* to move price reveal a passive
  counterparty soaking them up (see [CVD](/learn/cumulative-volume-delta)).
- **Liquidations** — forced market orders can produce sudden one-sided blocks.

## What block trades can't tell you

Be honest about the limits:

- **Trades aren't intent.** A block is a fact about size, not a prediction.
- **Side is still inferred** — same caveat as all
  [order flow](/learn/what-is-order-flow).
- **Resting games are invisible.** Spoofed or hidden orders never print on the tape.
- **Aggregation differs.** Some venues report one large fill; others split it into many
  smaller prints, which can hide a "block" or fake one.

A single whale print is a data point, not a thesis. Combine it with tape context, CVD, and
the depth book before reading too much into it.
