# Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD), Explained

**Cumulative Volume Delta** turns raw order flow into a single running line that tracks
whether aggressive buyers or aggressive sellers are winning over time.

## Step 1: delta

For any trade, assign its volume a sign based on the aggressor:

- Aggressive **buy** → `+volume`
- Aggressive **sell** → `−volume`

**Delta** for a window is simply the sum: `buy volume − sell volume`. A positive delta
means takers were net buyers in that window; negative means net sellers.

## Step 2: cumulative

**CVD** is the running total of delta across trades. Plotted over time it rises when
buyers dominate and falls when sellers dominate — a compact picture of net initiative.

## Reading CVD against price

CVD is most useful *compared to price*:

- **Confirmation** — price up *and* CVD up: the move is backed by aggressive buying.
- **Divergence** — price makes a new high but CVD doesn't: the rally is losing initiative;
  buyers are getting weaker even as price holds.
- **Absorption** — CVD falls hard (heavy selling) but price barely moves: a passive buyer
  is *absorbing* the sells. Often more meaningful than the headline price.

Divergences are hints, not signals. They describe what *has* happened, not what *will*.

## The caveats nobody mentions

- **Side is inferred.** CVD inherits all the uncertainty of
  [aggressor inference](/learn/what-is-order-flow). It is an approximation.
- **Cross-venue CVD is apples-to-oranges.** Each exchange has different participants and
  liquidity; summing their CVDs blurs more than it reveals. Compare per-venue.
- **Windowing matters.** A 30-second window and a full-session accumulation tell different
  stories. Know which one you're reading.
- **No book context.** CVD says nothing about resting liquidity — pair it with the
  [depth ladder](/learn/reading-the-trade-matrix).
