# What Is Order Flow?

**Order flow** is the real-time stream of activity that moves a market: the trades that
actually execute (the *tape*) and the resting orders waiting to be filled (the *order
book*). Reading it is about answering one question — *who is initiating, buyers or
sellers, and how aggressively?*

## The two sides: makers and takers

Every trade has two participants:

- A **maker** posts a resting limit order on the book and waits.
- A **taker** sends a market order that crosses the spread and executes immediately
  against the maker's resting order.

The taker is the **aggressor** — the one *demanding* liquidity right now. When a taker
buys, they "lift the offer"; when they sell, they "hit the bid." Order flow analysis is
largely about tracking aggressor activity over time.

## Why it matters

Price is the *result*; order flow is the *cause*. By watching the tape you can often see:

- **Initiative** — is one side repeatedly paying up to get filled?
- **Speed** — is the tape accelerating (interest building) or going quiet?
- **Size** — are prints small and retail-like, or large and deliberate?
- **Absorption** — heavy selling that *fails* to push price down means someone is quietly
  buying everything offered. That's information.

## Inference, not certainty

Here's the honest part most tools skip. From a public trade feed you don't get traders'
intentions — you get prices, sizes, and a flag. Order Flow Matrix infers the aggressor
side from each exchange's maker/taker flag (the standard approach), but:

- Inferred side is an approximation, not ground truth.
- A single venue's tape is not the whole market.
- Trades don't reveal resting-order games like spoofing.

Treat order flow as a high-quality *signal*, not an oracle. (And nothing here is financial
advice — see the [disclaimer](/disclaimer).)
